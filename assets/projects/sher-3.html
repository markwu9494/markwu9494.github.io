Overview

The Steady-Hand Eye Robot (SHER) is a cooperatively controlled assistant for vitreoretinal microsurgery developed at Johns Hopkins University. In SHER 3.0, I contributed to mechatronics design, prototyping, and kinematics optimization for a compact parallel mechanism optimized for subretinal injection.

Highlights:

  • Cooperative admittance control filters physiological tremor while preserving surgeon intent
  • Compact parallel RCM architecture improves OR ergonomics for left- or right-handed surgeons
  • Benchtop evaluation demonstrates feasibility for subretinal injection tasks
  • IROS 2020 paper on optimized tilt mechanism selected as Best Paper Finalist

Mechanism & Benchtop Setup

SHER 3.0 integrates a surgical microscope, compact robot body, and cooperative control interface. The system is designed for space-constrained operating rooms while maintaining sub-millimeter positioning accuracy.

Evaluation

Benchtop experiments validated robot responsiveness, ergonomic usability, and precision during subretinal injection proxy tasks on phantom eye models.
